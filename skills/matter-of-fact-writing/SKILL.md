# Matter of Fact Writing

State what happened, what the evidence shows, what changed, and what was concluded. Do not add emotional significance or rhetorical emphasis after the factual point is already clear.

## Write

- Prefer concrete claims over interpretation of those claims.
- State the sequence of reasoning directly: starting question, evidence, correction, conclusion.
- Name the person, action, object, or decision when a pronoun or abstraction would be unclear.
- Distinguish facts, quotations, interpretations, and conclusions.
- Preserve uncertainty when the source is uncertain.
- Preserve quantitative scope, denominators, qualifiers, and words such as "most," "some," or "approximately."
- Preserve direct quotations unless the user asks to edit them.
- Let quotations carry their original emotion. Keep surrounding narration factual.
- Use headings only when they make a longer document easier to scan.
- Keep necessary context. Matter-of-fact does not mean terse, impersonal, or incomplete.

## Remove

- Sentences that announce importance instead of explaining it: "This matters because..."
- Dramatic contrasts when the practical difference can be stated directly.
- Motivational conclusions, aphorisms, and tidy closing insights.
- Metaphors added by the writer rather than present in the source.
- Claims about motives, emotions, or meaning that the evidence does not establish.
- Advice, next steps, or decisions that the source does not contain and the user did not request.
- Rhetorical bridge phrases such as "This changed everything," "The deeper truth," or "At its core."
- Repetition where a second sentence restates the first with more emphasis.
- Evaluative adjectives such as "powerful," "profound," "important," or "remarkable" unless they convey necessary sourced information.

## Revision Process

1. Identify the factual point in each paragraph.
2. Check whether every sentence adds evidence, context, reasoning, or a conclusion.
3. Delete sentences that only interpret the significance of the preceding sentence.
4. Replace abstractions with the actual action or conclusion.
5. Check attribution, numbers, denominators, qualifiers, and the source's level of certainty.
6. Remove recommendations or implied next steps introduced during rewriting.
7. Read the result for continuity. Restore context if compression made the reasoning difficult to follow.

## Examples

Before:

> The conclusion matters, but the path to it matters more. Without the path, Chris can easily reopen the same question, forget what he learned, and begin searching for certainty again.

After:

> This note preserves the reasoning behind the conclusion so Chris does not need to reconstruct it when he questions his direction.

Before:

> That made optionality feel less like freedom and more like weight.

After:

> Having many valid options made it harder for Chris to commit to any one of them.

Before:

> The video is a second completion.

After:

> A project has two outputs: the thing itself and the finished video.

Before:

> This preserves room for ambition without making revenue the test of whether the project mattered.

After:

> A project can be worthwhile without generating revenue. Demand may justify producing or selling it later.

Before:

> The coherent thread is Chris.

After:

> The common thread is Chris's combination of taste, practical problem-solving, design, making, and hosting.

## Final Check

Confirm that the prose:

- states rather than performs insight;
- includes the evidence needed to support its conclusions;
- does not invent significance, motivation, or certainty;
- does not add advice or alter the scope of quantitative claims;
- retains the author's actual position and relevant nuance;
- stops after the final useful fact or conclusion.
